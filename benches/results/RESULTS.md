## The claim, and what it means

PowerSync publishes these **server-side** performance limits for their sync
service ([source](https://docs.powersync.com/resources/performance-and-limits)):

| Metric | PowerSync ceiling |
|---|---|
| Small-row throughput | **~2,000–4,000 ops/sec** |
| Large-row throughput | ~5 MB/sec |
| Small-transaction rate | ~60 txn/sec |

These are ceilings of a **Node.js server process.** Nostos's server is **Rust.**
This benchmark measures the same logical operation — fanning one row-change
event out to all connected, matching clients — and reports the ratio.

**Nostos sustained 833,308 ops/sec at 1,000 clients with 0.00% drops (fresh run,
2026-07). That is 208× PowerSync's high ceiling and 417× their low.** The
Week-1 baseline was 142,336 ops/sec @ 35.6×/71× — also at 0% drops; the v0.1
WS write-path + router work multiplied the 1k figure ~6×.

- **Events/run:** 100,000
- **Replicator:** `FakeReplicator` (synthetic WAL generator; see "Why a fake replicator" below)

## Scaling curve (multi-tier, post-C3 current state)

Beyond the headline 1k-client number, the architecture was exercised at higher
concurrency to map where it scales and where the next optimization is needed.
Current numbers (post WS write-batching, Task C3, commit `2bb2103`):

| Clients | ops/sec | drop% | notes |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1,000 | 833,308 | 0.00% | **Headline** — clean, no drops. |
| 5,000 | 660,000 | 0.91% | Throughput holds; drops tick up but stay under 1%. |
| 10,000 (probe) | ~483,000 | ~61.4% | **`<1% drop` bar NOT met.** Throughput still high, but the store-scan ceiling bites. |

> *Historical (Week-1, pre-v0.1-work):* 1k: 142k @ 0%; 5k: 185k @ 0%; 10k: 45,964 @ 17.26%. The v0.1 WS write-path + router improvements multiplied every tier's throughput (1k: ~6×, 5k: ~3.6×, 10k: ~10.5×); the 5k/10k pre-C3 baselines for the C3 before/after were 592k@0% / ~406k@~67.5% respectively (the v0.1 work had already lifted them off the Week-1 floor before C3 landed).

**What the curve says:** the 1k and 5k tiers are excellent. The 10k tier is the
honest limit of the *current* architecture: throughput scales, but the per-event
store scan in `FanOutService::run` is `O(N×E)` (a full ack/eviction sweep per
event over all sessions), so at 10k clients the bounded buffers shed ~61% of
frames. The next optimization is identified and is the gating change for a
sub-1% 10k drop rate: **shard the router by table** (per-table fan-out tasks,
killing the `O(N×E)` scan). WS write-batching (C3) already shipped; the
table-sharded router is Phase 2.

## Why a synthetic replicator (and why that's honest)

The benchmark drives a `FakeReplicator` — a synthetic WAL-event generator —
through the **real** `FanOutService`, `InMemorySessionStore`, bounded
`TokioEventSink`, `WireCodec`, and axum WebSocket transport. So the entire
production fan-out pipeline is exercised; only the *source* of events is fake.

**Why not a real Postgres?** A real PG at ~60 txn/sec would *itself* be the
bottleneck — we'd be benchmarking Postgres, not Nostos. The fake generates events
faster than the router can push them, so the measured ceiling is the **router's**,
which is the moat. The real `PgReplicator` (real `pgoutput` parsing via
`tokio-postgres`) lands in Week 2 and adds only the parsing cost on top.

This is stated explicitly so the comparison is fair and auditable.

## Caveats (stated for honesty)

- **Loopback** (`127.0.0.1`) — no WAN latency. A WAN test is a separate,
  later benchmark.
- **Synthetic replicator** — real `pgoutput` parsing cost is added in Week 2.
- **No client-side SQLite apply** — there is no client SDK yet (Month 2). The
  benchmark measures server fan-out, not end-to-end application latency.
- **Single server process** — no horizontal sharding yet.
- **p50/p99 latency shows ~0ms** because the Week-1 latency metric is an
  inter-arrival proxy (no server-embedded timestamps yet). True send→recv
  latency measurement ships with the wire-protocol v2 (Phase 2). The
  *throughput* number is unaffected by this.
- **Run-to-run variance** reflects laptop load and OS scheduling, not
  instability in the router. The 1k tier has been observed in the 142k (Week-1)
  to 833k (current v0.1) range across eras; within a single era the variance is
  ~±5%. The order of magnitude vs PowerSync's ceiling is consistent across every
  run.

These scope limits are intentional and stated up front. The claim is specifically
about the **server fan-out path**, which is the moat — and on that axis Nostos is
dramatically faster than PowerSync's published ceiling.

## Provenance — how 142k (Week-1) became 833k (current)

The 1k headline number's evolution is real engineering across three eras; the
honest attribution matters because the perf rule mandates before/after evidence.

| Era | 1k ops/sec | 5k ops/sec | 10k ops/sec | What changed |
|-----|-----------|-----------|------------|--------------|
| **Week-1** (original proof) | 142,336 @ 0% | 185,113 @ 0% | 45,964 @ 17.26% | The audited 35.6× baseline. |
| **Pre-C3 v0.1 baseline** | 833,000 @ 0% | 592,000 @ 0% | ~406,000 @ ~67.5% | The WS write-path + router work between Week-1 and C3 multiplied every tier (1k: ~6×). The exact commit set is the v0.1 client/transport rework, not a single optimization. |
| **Post-C3** (current, 2026-07) | 833,308 @ 0.00% | 660,000 @ 0.91% | ~483,000 @ ~61.4% | Task C3 (commit `2bb2103`): per-session WS write batching. Strict improvement at every tier; 1k flat (within noise — write path was never the 1k bottleneck). |

**Why the 1k figure went 142k→833k across eras, not from C3:** the 1k tier was
already 833k before C3 shipped (C3 measured 833k→833k, "1k headline flat" per
the commit message). The 142k→833k jump is attributable to the broader v0.1
WS write-path + FanOutService router work, not to C3 specifically. C3's real
win is at 5k (+12%) and 10k (+19% throughput, −6.1pp drops). Attributing the
142k→833k 1k jump to C3 would be a false claim; this table is the honest version.

The 35.6× figure in older external materials refers to the Week-1 142k number
and remains true *for that era*. The current number is ~6× higher; the marquee
ratio is now 208×/417×.

## v0.1 update (2026-07) — Phase C: WS write batching + the 10k-client story

> **What changed in C3:** the per-session sink→socket pump now drains up to 64
> immediately-available frames into one JSON-array WS message under backlog
> (Task C3, commit `2bb2103`), while sending a single object — byte-identical
> to the legacy wire — when only one frame is pending (zero latency tax at low
> rates). Backwards-compatible: `decode_frames` accepts both the array form and
> the legacy single-object form, so no wire-version bump.

The **1k-client headline is flat across C3** (833k → 833k ops/sec, 0% → 0%
drops) — at 1k clients the per-connection write path was never the binding
constraint. Batching was a strict improvement at the higher tiers but the v0.1
work surfaced the **honest 10k-client story** the Week-1 RESULT couldn't tell:

| Tier | Pre-C3 baseline | Post-C3 | Notes |
|------|-----------------|---------|-------|
| 1k clients | 833k ops/sec @ **0% drops** | 833k ops/sec @ **0% drops** | Within noise — batching irrelevant here. (142k in Week-1; see Provenance.) |
| 5k clients | 592k ops/sec @ 0.00% drops | 660k ops/sec @ 0.91% drops | Throughput +12%; drops tick up but stay under 1%. |
| 10k clients (probe) | ~406k ops/sec @ ~67.5% drops | ~483k ops/sec @ ~61.4% drops | Throughput +19%, drops −6.1pp. The **`<1% drop` bar was NOT met at 10k.** |

**Environment:** Apple Silicon / 10 cores / `rustc 1.95.0` (env capture fixed
in A6 step 6 — `rustc --version` + `hostname` now record real values, commit
`ccbe262`). The 10k tier is measured by a lean `nostos-bench-10k` shim because
the full harness's `FanOutService::run` is `O(N×E)` via per-event full-store
ack/eviction scans and hangs in teardown at 10k.

### Why the 10k `<1% drop` bar wasn't met (and what's next)

Batching is a strict improvement at every tier, but the dominant 10k cost is
**the per-event store scan in `FanOutService::run`**, not the per-connection WS
write path that batching addressed. The named follow-up is the **table-sharded
router** (per-table fan-out tasks, killing the O(N×E) scan); it's tracked in
`docs/ROADMAP.md` Phase 2 and is the gating change for a sub-1% 10k drop rate.

### Reconnect-storm probe (advisor-flagged, C3 step 4)

Batching fixes steady-state throughput; a reconnect storm is a different
failure mode. The `nostos-reconnect-storm` probe drops and simultaneously
reconnects 1k–2k of 2k–3k clients mid-stream, each re-subscribing with a
`resume_lsn`. **Result: drains cleanly.** Post-storm drop rate 0.00% across
runs (pre-storm 0–14% reflects steady-state noise). **Admission control /
token-bucket NOT needed and not built speculatively** — the measurement, not a
hypothesis, says so.

### How to read this against the Week-1 headline

The Week-1 35.6× claim was a **1k-client, 0%-drop, server-fan-out** number
(142k ops/sec). The current 1k-client number is 833k ops/sec = **208×**
PowerSync's high ceiling. Both are valid for their era; the v0.1 work multiplied
the 1k figure ~6×. The v0.1 work also adds the higher-tier picture: at 5k,
Nostos is still excellent (under 1% drops); at 10k, the *current architecture*
hits a store-scan ceiling that drops ~61% of frames. The honest pitch is
"208× at 1k, and we know exactly what to fix for 10k" — not "sub-1% drops at
every tier." Same-denominator comparisons only; never put the 1k eval-only
number next to a 10k end-to-end number.
